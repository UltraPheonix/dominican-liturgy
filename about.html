About this site

This website is dedicated to Peter Bratschi, Josef Crenshaw, and Father Gregory Santy, who have both put up with me, and have all worked diligently to bring the Dominican rite to Providence College. This would not have been possible without them.

This site shows the sung propers of the Dominican Rite Mass (Introit, Gradual, Alleluia or Tract, Sequence, Offertory, Communion) for any day, in square notation, from the Graduale juxta ritum Sacri Ordinis Praedicatorum (Rome, 1950, ed. Suarez). Each chant can be changed, edited as GABC, printed or saved as a PDF, and shared as a link.

Where the chants come from

All the chants of the 1950 Graduale were transcribed into GABC by volunteers for GregoBase; each chant on this site names its transcriber and links to its GregoBase page. One chant (the Alleluia Laetatus sum, p. 5) is still empty there.

How each day's Mass was put together

The Graduale prints each chant once, in the order of the year, and refers back to it when it is used again. So every Mass was assembled from three books and checked against each other:

  • Proper of the Time. Each Mass of the Graduale was matched to its day by its Introit, then checked against the St. Dominic Missal (1959), which supplies any chant the Graduale only refers back to.
  • Proper of the Saints. The feasts, dates and ranks are those of the Calendarium O.P. (1960 rubrics). The chants are those the St. Dominic Missal assigns ("Mass: No. 13 except…" gives the Common and the proper parts), or, where it could not be read, the page of the Missale O.P. (1939) that the 2026 Ordo cites.
  • The Mass of the day is chosen by class, as in the rubrics of 1960, and agrees with the 2026 Ordo O.P. on the days it was checked against. Saturdays without a feast have the Mass of Our Lady.
  • Seasons. From Septuagesima a saint's Alleluia gives way to the Tract; in Paschaltide the Gradual gives way to two Alleluias, and the optional T.P. Alleluia is sung only then.
  • Special days. Ember days have their extra lessons, each followed by its gradual; the Rogation days and the Vigils have no Gloria and no Alleluia; the chants of the blessings of candles (2 February), ashes and palms come before the Mass; Holy Week follows the Missal and the Ceremonial of 1877 (Maundy Thursday, the Mass of the Presanctified with the Adoration of the Cross, Holy Saturday with its prophecies, no Agnus Dei, and the short Vespers after the Communion).

What the site decides for you

Choose a day and the page shows the Mass exactly as it is to be sung, in order. Nothing has to be set by hand:

  • Asperges or Vidi aquam before the principal Mass on Sundays (Vidi aquam from Easter to Pentecost).
  • Kyriale by the rank of the day, after the Kyriale O.P. as used since 1960: Mass I on feasts of the first class, II on Sundays, III on feasts of the second class, VI on feasts of the third class, V on feasts and Saturdays of Our Lady, and the ferial Mass (Profestis diebus) on ferias and days of penance; in Paschaltide the Easter Kyrie on Sundays and greater feasts. Masses for the Dead have the Requiem Kyrie and Requiescant in pace.
  • Gloria and Credo by the rubrics of 1960, and Ite, missa est at every Mass (with Alleluia in the Easter octave); Benedicamus Domino only on Maundy Thursday, when the procession follows.
  • Gradual, Alleluia, Tract and Sequence by the season and the kind of day.
  • The Preface proper to the feast or the season (or the Common Preface), with its chanted dialogue in the solemn or the ferial tone, and the variable words of the Preface of Our Lady and of Easter filled in.

These choices have been checked against every day of the 2026 Ordo O.P.: the Gloria, the Credo, the Preface and the colour agree on 363–364 of the 365 days. The few differences are days where the Ordo itself chooses between two Masses. You can still change the Kyriale, Credo or Asperges with the menus.

The English

Readings are from the Vulgate with the Douay-Rheims. For a chant whose words are taken straight from Scripture, the English is the Douay-Rheims of the same words, matched clause by clause. Chants that are not word for word from the Vulgate (the Old Latin psalter, the sequences, the texts of the feasts) have our own translation, close to the Latin and in the same style.

A chant marked Roman melody is not in the Graduale O.P. on GregoBase and is shown from the Roman Graduale until it is transcribed. A few saints whose proper chants are not yet transcribed (St Thérèse, the Guardian Angels) take the chants of the Common, and the page says so.

Printing

Choir PDF prints the chants of the Mass (and the Kyriale, if chosen) in square notation. Congregation PDF prints every proper and reading in Latin with the English beside it. Both open your device's print dialog: choose “Save as PDF”.

The Divine Office

The Office page gives every Hour, Matins to Compline, in Latin and English for each day. It follows the Breviarium juxta ritum Sacri Ordinis Prædicatorum (1962) and the 1960 rubrics. The day's text is produced with the engine and the Ordo Praedicatorum – 1962 data of Divinum Officium (MIT licence), whose English it uses, and is checked against a scan of the 1962 Breviary. Where a Dominican text has no English yet, the Latin stands alone for now. The Office is spoken for now; the chant will follow.

Disclaimer

This is a private project. It is not affiliated with, approved by or endorsed by the Order of Preachers (the Dominican Order) or any of its provinces, and it is not an official liturgical book. Check it against the Ordo of your house or province before you rely on it.

The sources do not always agree: the Missal of 1939 and the Graduale of 1950 predate the reforms of 1960, which changed the calendar, the ranks of feasts and some rubrics. Wherever they conflict, this site follows the calendar and rubrics of 1960 (as in the 2026 Ordo O.P.), and uses the older books for the texts and chants.
